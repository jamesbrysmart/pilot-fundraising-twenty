# Fundraising for Twenty

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary audience is the nonprofit buying group responsible for improving or replacing the fundraising CRM at a small-to-mid-sized organisation. In practice, this may be one person wearing several hats or a small group spanning fundraising, operations and senior leadership. It includes fundraising managers and directors, operations or CRM leads, and CEOs or senior managers who directly own or influence the decision.

They need to understand whether Fundraising for Twenty supports real fundraising work, whether migration and implementation are manageable, and whether the wider CRM can be shaped around the organisation.

The secondary audience includes data, finance and technically minded stakeholders involved in due diligence; existing or prospective Twenty users; and Twenty ecosystem or marketplace reviewers assessing product credibility, extensibility and implementation maturity.

The page must support two reading speeds. Senior decision-makers should quickly understand the strategic case, credibility and next step. Fundraising and operational users should be able to inspect workflow evidence, flexibility, migration and implementation detail.

## Product Purpose

Help nonprofit decision-makers understand why Fundraising for Twenty offers a practical alternative to both rigid fundraising systems and starting with an empty general CRM or building the whole fundraising system from scratch. Visitors should be able to assess whether it fits their fundraising and wider CRM needs, trust that it is a credible product available now, and take a low-pressure next step toward tailored implementation with 3Trees Digital.

Success follows this hierarchy:

1. Visitors quickly understand the core proposition.
2. They see enough real product and workflow evidence to believe it.
3. They understand that the app is free for nonprofits, while Twenty and implementation costs are separate.
4. They feel that migration, setup and adoption are manageable.
5. They know what next action to take.

The page optimizes first for confident product evaluation and trust, then for enquiry rather than aggressive immediate conversion.

## Positioning

Fundraising for Twenty combines a developed fundraising app with Twenty's flexible CRM foundation and practical, tailored implementation support. It gives nonprofits a substantial starting point without locking them into a rigid fundraising system or requiring them to begin with an empty general CRM and commission the whole fundraising system from the ground up.

## Conversion & Proof

The primary action is **Tell us about your organisation**, which opens the structured enquiry. It should receive the strongest emphasis without creating pressure.

The secondary action is **What to expect**, a genuinely visible evaluation route for visitors who want more confidence about fit, implementation, costs and next steps before enquiring. The tertiary fallback is **Contact us** for an unstructured or specific question that does not fit the enquiry form.

The line a visitor should remember after ten seconds is:

> Start with a developed fundraising app. Shape the wider CRM around your nonprofit.

The belief sequence is:

1. Fundraising for Twenty is a developed, available product rather than a blank CRM configuration.
2. Its real workflows support substantial fundraising work.
3. Twenty's flexibility allows the wider CRM to be shaped around the organisation.
4. The free app, separate Twenty costs and tailored implementation model are clear.
5. Migration, setup and adoption can be practical and manageable with 3Trees Digital.
6. A low-pressure enquiry is the appropriate next step.

The main proof available today is the functioning product itself:

- Five real workflow screenshots covering donor records, donation processing, recurring giving, fundraising pipelines and dashboards. These are evidence, not decoration.
- The online donation form and its connection to the wider fundraising workflow, demonstrating donor-facing collection and internal administration.
- Concrete fundraising structures and workflows including gifts, recurring agreements, appeals, funds, donor records and staged donation processing.
- Donor-administration workflows intended to reduce errors, duplicate records and messy data through donor matching, staged review and controlled processing before donations become committed records.
- Integration capability through Twenty's extensible platform, including the ability to connect with payment providers, imports and other systems. Claims must not imply that every integration already exists.
- Working demonstrations during initial conversations with suitable organisations.
- 3Trees Digital's nonprofit CRM, data migration and implementation experience. This supports confidence in delivery but must not imply that Fundraising for Twenty has already been widely deployed.
- Real nonprofit implementation and testing experience, described generally unless a client explicitly approves being named.
- A genuine technical relationship to Twenty: the product is built on Twenty CRM, but is not an official Twenty product and must not imply endorsement or partnership.
- Directness about availability, costs, implementation and maturity as a trust signal.

There are currently no approved testimonials, quantified customer outcomes, published case studies, customer logos, official Twenty endorsement, or confirmed marketplace presence or traction. Do not invent or imply them.

## Brand Personality

The visual personality is **calm, capable and considered**.

- Calm: restrained, reassuring and free from urgency or startup noise.
- Capable: credible, substantial and technically assured.
- Considered: deliberate hierarchy, careful spacing and purposeful interaction rather than decoration.

The desired emotional outcome is for suitable visitors to feel informed, reassured and quietly confident that changing CRM could be manageable, and that they do not have to choose between a rigid fundraising product and an overwhelming build from scratch. They should feel clear about the product, reassured about migration and adoption, confident in its availability and credibility, and optimistic that a better-fit CRM is achievable.

Communication should be candid even when the visual tone is quiet: be direct about costs, implementation, scope and product maturity.

The focused reference set is:

- Twenty for product grounding, interface character and the sense of a modern, extensible CRM, without copying its marketing site.
- Notion for approachable clarity, generous whitespace and a calm presentation of flexibility without unnecessary technical complexity.
- Linear for precision, strong hierarchy and polished product presentation, without adopting a dark developer-tool aesthetic or excessive motion.

The combined direction is Twenty's product grounding, Notion's approachable clarity and Linear's precision.

## Anti-references

The experience should not become:

- A generic nonprofit site that relies on soft colors, friendly illustrations and emotional campaign language while underplaying the product, workflows and implementation reality.
- A loud startup or SaaS page with gradients, oversized claims, urgency, decorative motion and conversion pressure.
- A dark developer-tool aesthetic that makes the product feel technical, exclusive or inaccessible.
- A corporate CRM consultancy site dominated by abstract promises, service language and generic transformation claims.
- An overly editorial or typographic concept that looks sophisticated but hides the product evidence.
- A feature-heavy CRM page with too many cards, labels and capability inventories competing for attention.

The page should feel warm enough for nonprofit visitors while remaining product-led, operationally credible and visually disciplined.

## Design Principles

1. **Optimize for confident evaluation before conversion.** Encourage enquiry by making the product understandable and credible, not by applying urgency or pressure.
2. **Let real product evidence carry the claims.** Make screenshots, workflows and demonstrations legible, contextual and central to the story.
3. **Communicate the developed core and flexible wider CRM together.** Tailored implementation is part of the value; the distinction is that organisations do not start from an empty CRM or build the fundraising system from scratch.
4. **Be direct about adoption, costs and maturity.** Precision and candour create trust and prevent product, platform or partnership claims from being overstated.
5. **Support fast strategic scanning and deeper operational inspection.** Strengthen hierarchy without stripping away the implementation and workflow detail that establishes credibility.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA is the minimum standard. Priorities include:

- Clear contrast and comfortably readable body text.
- Full keyboard access and visible focus states.
- Reduced-motion support.
- Forms, dialogs and side panels that work correctly with assistive technology.
- Comfortable reading and interaction at mobile sizes and browser zoom.
- No reliance on color alone to communicate meaning.
- Clear labels, instructions and validation for the enquiry form.
- Straightforward navigation and familiar interaction patterns for visitors without specialist technical knowledge.
- Product and implementation information that remains understandable without hover interactions, animation or dense technical language.

The audience may include small nonprofit teams with limited CRM or technical expertise, so accessibility includes cognitive clarity as well as technical compliance.
